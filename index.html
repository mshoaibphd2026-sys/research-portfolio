About Me

I earned my Master’s degree in Physical Chemistry from the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences (UCAS), conducting my research at the Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics (DICP), Chinese Academy of Sciences, within the State Key Laboratory of Catalysis. My research focused on the synthesis and characterization of metal oxide semiconductors for light-driven catalytic oxidation reactions. I am currently seeking a Ph.D. position in the field of nanomaterials, with particular interest in their applications in energy conversion and storage.

Research Theme

Photocatalytic Organic Transformation

Selective light-driven conversion of organic molecules using semiconductor catalysts and molecular oxygen.

Materials Design

0D/1D Heterostructures

Hydrothermal synthesis enables controlled growth of semiconductor nanostructures under elevated temperature and pressure. In this work, Na2Ti3O7 nanotubes served as the 1D support for the in situ growth of Bi2MoO6 nanoclusters, forming a 0D/1D heterostructure.

Mechanistic Study

Charge Transport & Mechanism

Radical trapping experiments were used to identify the active species involved in the photocatalytic oxidation process and to support the proposed charge transfer pathway. The scavenger results, together with the mechanistic model, help explain the catalytic behavior of the heterostructure.

Master's Thesis Project

0D/1D Bi2MoO6/Na2Ti3O7 Heterostructure for Blue-Light Photooxidation

Published in ACS Applied Nano Materials • First Author

Designed an in-situ hydrothermally synthesized 0D/1D Bi2MoO6/Na2Ti3O7 heterostructure for the selective blue-light-driven oxidation of benzyl alcohol to benzaldehyde.

Representative result: the optimized BMT-10 catalyst achieved 6.8 mmolconverted BzOH gcat−1 h−1.
