Collaborative Research Project

BiFeO₃/Na₂Ti₃O₇ p–n Heterostructure for Blue-Light-Driven Photooxidation

This collaborative project investigated a 0D/1D bismuth ferrite/sodium titanate heterostructure designed to improve visible-light photocatalysis through interfacial charge separation and controlled charge-carrier dynamics.

Published in Applied Catalysis B: Environment and Energy • Co-Authored Research

Research Objective

The project focused on coupling BiFeO₃ clusters with Na₂Ti₃O₇ nanotubes to construct a p–n heterostructured photocatalyst for the selective oxidation of benzyl alcohol under blue-light irradiation. The central scientific question was how semiconductor coupling influences photoinduced charge separation, carrier relaxation, and the resulting photocatalytic behavior.

My Contribution

Ultrafast Spectroscopy Analysis

Analyzed femtosecond transient absorption (fs-TA) spectra to compare excited-state behavior in pristine Na₂Ti₃O₇ and the BiFeO₃/Na₂Ti₃O₇ heterostructure.

Charge-Carrier Dynamics

Interpreted transient-decay behavior to distinguish rapid carrier processes, interfacial charge transfer/recombination, and slower relaxation pathways.

Mechanistic Correlation

Correlated fs-TA observations with complementary photophysical measurements, including time-resolved photoluminescence, to support the proposed charge-transfer mechanism.

Scientific Communication

Contributed to interpretation, scientific visualization, and manuscript development for the collaborative publication.

Charge-Carrier Dynamics

Femtosecond transient absorption analysis used to investigate photoinduced carrier relaxation and interfacial charge-transfer behavior.

The transient spectroscopic analysis helped resolve how the heterointerface altered the lifetime and relaxation pathways of photoexcited carriers. These observations were used together with other optical and electrochemical evidence to understand the improved charge-separation behavior of the composite photocatalyst.

Research Significance

The study demonstrates how rational heterointerface construction can be used not only to improve photocatalytic performance, but also to control the fate of photogenerated electrons and holes. Understanding these ultrafast processes is important for designing semiconductor photocatalysts with more efficient charge utilization in light-driven chemical transformations.
